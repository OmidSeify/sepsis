# Neonatal Sepsis and Meningitis

---

## Objectives
- Define early-onset and late-onset neonatal sepsis
- Review epidemiology, risk factors, and common pathogens
- Recognize clinical presentation and red flags
- Outline diagnostic evaluation (labs, cultures, LP)
- Present empiric and targeted antimicrobial therapy
- Discuss supportive care and complications
- Summarize neonatal meningitis: diagnosis, management, and outcomes
- Prevention strategies

---

## Definitions & Classification
- Neonatal Sepsis: systemic infection occurring in the first 28 days of life
- Early-Onset Sepsis (EOS): ≤72 hours of life (some references ≤7 days)
- Late-Onset Sepsis (LOS): >72 hours (or >7 days) to 28 days (often hospital-acquired in preterm infants)
- Meningitis: infection of meninges; frequently complicates bacteremia in neonates

Notes: Clarify EOS/LOS cutoff as per local protocol; using EOS ≤72 hours.

---

## Epidemiology
- Higher incidence in preterm and very low birth weight (VLBW) infants
- EOS typically linked to maternal genitourinary colonization and vertical transmission
- LOS often associated with nosocomial sources (lines/devices) or community exposure

Notes: Emphasize higher risk in VLBW and preterm infants.

---

## Risk Factors
- EOS:
  - Maternal: Group B Streptococcus (GBS) colonization, chorioamnionitis/intra-amniotic infection, fever, prolonged rupture of membranes (PROM), intrapartum fever, inadequate intrapartum prophylaxis
  - Neonatal: prematurity, low birth weight, male sex
- LOS:
  - Central lines (UVC/UAC, PICC), endotracheal tubes/ventilation
  - TPN use, prolonged hospitalization, environmental exposure

Notes: Use local definitions of chorioamnionitis and IAI.

---

## Common Pathogens
- EOS:
  - GBS (Streptococcus agalactiae)
  - Escherichia coli (especially in preterm)
  - Other Gram-negatives (Klebsiella, Enterobacter)
- LOS:
  - Coagulase-negative Staphylococci (CONS)
  - Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA)
  - Gram-negatives: E. coli, Klebsiella, Pseudomonas
  - Candida spp. (especially with TPN/antibiotics/lines)
- Meningitis:
  - EOS: GBS, E. coli
  - LOS: CONS less commonly causes meningitis; GNRs, S. aureus, Listeria (rare), Candida

Notes: Tailor to local antibiogram.

---

## Clinical Presentation
- Non-specific: temperature instability (hypothermia or fever), lethargy, irritability, poor feeding
- Respiratory: tachypnea, grunting, apnea, increased oxygen requirement
- Cardiovascular: tachycardia/bradycardia, poor perfusion, hypotension
- Neurologic: seizures, bulging fontanelle (meningitis), abnormal tone
- GI: vomiting, abdominal distention, feeding intolerance
- Metabolic: hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, acidosis
- Jaundice (sepsis-associated)

Red flags:
- Apnea/bradycardia in preterm
- Rapid deterioration
- Focal signs: cellulitis, omphalitis, osteoarticular infection

Notes: Keep a high index of suspicion; signs are subtle.

---

## Diagnostic Evaluation
- Immediate stabilization, then sepsis workup:
  - Blood culture (≥1; consider 2 if feasible)
  - CBC with differential; platelets
  - CRP and/or procalcitonin (trend more useful than single value)
  - Basic metabolic panel; glucose; lactate
  - Urine culture (typically for LOS; catheterized specimen)
  - Lumbar puncture (LP) if clinically stable and no contraindications
    - CSF: cell count, protein, glucose, Gram stain, culture; consider CSF PCR where available
- Additional:
  - Chest X-ray if respiratory symptoms
  - Line cultures if LOS and central access present

Notes: If unstable, defer LP until stabilized but obtain antibiotics promptly.

---

## CSF Interpretation (Neonates)
- Normal neonatal CSF differs from older children:
  - WBC: often <20 cells/µL (premature may have slightly higher)
  - Protein: higher baseline than older children (e.g., 60–150 mg/dL; prematurity increases)
  - Glucose: ~60–80% of simultaneous blood glucose
- Meningitis indicators:
  - Pleocytosis with neutrophilic predominance (bacterial), low CSF glucose, high protein
  - Positive Gram stain/culture or PCR

Notes: Interpret CSF in the context of gestational age and timing.

## Empiric Antibiotic Therapy
- EOS:
  - Ampicillin + Gentamicin
  - Consider broadening for severe illness or high local GNR resistance
- LOS:
  - Vancomycin + Gentamicin (or cefotaxime/ceftazidime depending on local ecology)
  - Add antipseudomonal coverage if risk factors (e.g., ceftazidime or piperacillin-tazobactam per NICU protocol)
  - Consider antifungal (e.g., amphotericin B) if high Candida risk (VLBW, TPN, prolonged antibiotics)
- Meningitis:
  - EOS meningitis: Ampicillin + Cefotaxime (avoid ceftriaxone in neonates) or Ampicillin + Gentamicin with cefotaxime substitution for CNS penetration
  - LOS meningitis: Vancomycin + a 3rd-gen cephalosporin (cefotaxime/ceftazidime) or tailored to local resistance
- Tailor therapy to culture/susceptibilities and local antibiogram

Notes: Avoid ceftriaxone in neonates due to bilirubin displacement and calcium precipitation risk. Dosing must follow neonatal weight/age-specific guidelines.

---

## Duration of Therapy
- Bacteremia without focus: typically 7–10 days (pathogen-dependent)
- Pneumonia: ~10 days
- Meningitis:
  - GBS and Listeria: ~14–21 days
  - Gram-negative bacilli: 21 days or longer; ensure CSF sterilization
- Complicated infections (osteomyelitis, endocarditis): longer, per specialist guidance

Notes: Reassess duration based on clinical course and culture clearance.

---

## Supportive Care
- Respiratory support (oxygen, CPAP/ventilation)
- Hemodynamic support: fluids, vasopressors if shock
- Glucose management; electrolyte correction
- Thermoregulation and minimal handling
- Nutritional support; consider early minimal enteral feeds if stable
- Source control: remove/replace infected lines if LOS
- Monitoring for complications: DIC, renal/hepatic dysfunction

Notes: Use standardized NICU bundles for line care and ventilation.

---

## Prevention Strategies
- Maternal:
  - Universal GBS screening at 35–37 weeks; intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP) for indicated cases
  - Manage chorioamnionitis promptly
- Neonatal/NICU:
  - Hand hygiene, line care bundles, antimicrobial stewardship
  - Judicious use of invasive devices; early removal when possible
  - Breast milk feeding when feasible; reduces LOS risk
- Environmental:
  - Cohorting during outbreaks; surveillance cultures as per policy

Notes: Align prevention with AAP/CDC guidance and local policies.

---

## Special Considerations
- Preterm/VLBW infants: atypical presentations; higher risk of Candida and Gram-negative LOS
- Listeria monocytogenes: consider if maternal exposure; treat with ampicillin + gentamicin
- HSV: may mimic sepsis—consider PCR and acyclovir in compatible presentations (vesicles, abnormal LFTs, seizures)
- Antibiotic stewardship: avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum use; narrow when cultures guide

Notes: Add local HSV testing and acyclovir thresholds.

## Complications & Outcomes
- Acute: shock, DIC, respiratory failure, seizures
- Meningitis: hydrocephalus, hearing loss, neurodevelopmental impairment
- Mortality higher in preterm and Gram-negative infections
- Long-term follow-up: audiology, neurodevelopmental assessments

Notes: Highlight need for structured follow-up.

## Case Vignettes
- EOS: Term infant with PROM 24h, maternal fever; respiratory distress at 6h
  - Workup: blood culture, CBC, CRP; empiric ampicillin + gentamicin; defer LP if unstable
- LOS: 28-week infant day 14 with apnea/bradycardia, central line
  - Workup: blood culture, line culture; vancomycin + ceftazidime; consider antifungal if risk

## Key Takeaways
- Maintain high suspicion—neonatal signs are subtle
- Obtain cultures before antibiotics when feasible; don’t delay therapy in unstable infants
- Empiric regimens differ for EOS vs LOS; adjust to local patterns
- LP is crucial when meningitis suspected; ensure safe timing
- Prevention via maternal GBS strategies and NICU bundles is critical
